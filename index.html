01 — Biography

Mahdis Golzar Kashani is an Iranian composer and pianist based in London, working at the meeting point of Persian poetry, visual art, and Western contemporary classical technique.

Her music has been broadcast on BBC Radio 3, Deutschland Radio, and NPO Klassieke, and performed at the Edinburgh International Festival, the Heidelberg Music Festival, the Fränkischer Sommer, the Dubrovnik Summer Festival, the Kennedy Center, Roulette, and National Sawdust, New York.

She has received commissions from the Sitkovetsky Trio, the Hoolan Ensemble, the Hypercube Ensemble, the Hermes Ensemble, and the Babylon Orchestra, as well as from individual virtuoso performers and international festivals.

She has received over ten international prizes and selections, including from the IAWM Search for New Music, Crossing Borders Music, the Sacrarium Competition, Opera Contempo, and the KLK New Music Festival, and her work has been presented at venues and festivals across Europe, North and South America, Asia, and Australia, including the Bozar Centre for Fine Arts in Brussels, the Bon Festival, and the Oxford Lieder Festival.

2025 · Quartet

Instrumentation

Duration

Premiered
Hoolan Ensemble, 2025
In societies where dictatorships take hold, atomization of the community becomes a key instrument of oppression. By dismantling social interactions, silencing independent voices, and weakening the sense of collective responsibility, people are turned into isolated and scattered fragments, unable to listen to or empathize with one another. In such an environment, enforced individualism replaces solidarity, paving the way for absolute domination.

Premiered

The final days of writing this piece, which is based on the local dances of four regions of Iran, coincided with the killing of the Iranian girl, Mahsa Amini, and the Women, Life, Freedom movement. Music Web International · December 2023

Reviewing And the Moses Drowned, Glyn Pursglove described the string quartet — dedicated to children lost in the Syrian war — as a deeply affecting work, calling it a memorable piece both for its compositional skill and its emotional weight.

Church Times · November 2023

Roderic Dunnett singled out The Phoenix at the Oxford International Song Festival, noting how the piece set Persian poetry within a distinctly Persian sound world — shurangiz alongside cello — for a haunting effect.

Read More ↗
I Care If You Listen · April 2023

Sofia Rocha highlighted And the Moses Drowned as the opening track of the IFCA album, praising its shift from grief-stricken opening chords into a searching, rhythmically vigorous climax.

Claire Seymour previewed The Phoenix, a song cycle inspired by Beethoven's An die ferne Geliebte setting Hafez, Rumi, and Saadi, noting its blend of European and Iranian classical traditions with improvisation on traditional Persian instruments.
